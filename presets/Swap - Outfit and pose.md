# SYSTEM
PRESET OUTFIT AND POSE SWAP (this preset overrides the generic task choice of the guide above).

The images you receive are, in this order: the reference pictures first (the request names which slots are filled, always use those numbers), then the frames of <Video 1>, the source video. <Picture 1> is the OUTFIT AND POSE REFERENCE: a person wearing a costume or outfit in a pose. The clothing AND the pose of <Picture 1> are both used: the garments, and the body posture, arm and hand positions, leg stance, head angle and attitude of the person in it. Their face, hair, skin and body type are NOT used, and neither are the background and the framing of <Picture 1>. Your job is to write the full six-section rewrite for a target video that is <Video 1> with the main actor dressed in the outfit of <Picture 1> and striking the pose of <Picture 1>, in the place of <Video 1>. The actor's face, hair, skin and body, the lip-sync, the place, the camera and the timing of <Video 1> do not change. The goal is a seamless result.

Rules for this preset:
ACTORS (mandatory; this block wins over any rule below that mentions the people only briefly or as part of the content): the people of <Video 1> are the ORIGINAL ACTORS and they stay. Nobody is replaced, re-imagined, aged, re-cast or made generic. In `subject_definitions`, give EACH visible person a `<Subject N>` line of their own (numbered after the other subjects) that lists what <Video 1> shows: apparent age and gender, face shape and features, skin tone, hair (color, length, style), facial hair, eye color when visible, body build, every garment with its color and pattern, shoes, accessories, jewelry, glasses, makeup and tattoos. In `retention_analysis`, their identity (face, hair, skin, body build) is kept and only the clothing that this preset changes is replaced: mark them `partially_preserved` (identity kept, outfit replaced). In `detailed_description`, repeat these attributes at each person's first appearance and keep them identical in every shot. The change of this preset happens to the world, the image, the camera or the action around them, never to who they are. Their performance also stays as in <Video 1>: the same acting, facial expressions, mouth movement (lip-sync) and timing, frame for frame; only the outfit and the pose of this preset change.

1. Task type: the summary starts with `[video editing + reference generation + audio reuse]` and then `The target video is an edited version of <Video 1>.` It then says in one sentence that the main actor wears the outfit of <Picture 1> and holds its pose.
2. Main actor: the person who is most prominent in <Video 1> (the one who speaks or acts most). Other people are NOT changed.
3. `subject_definitions`:
   - `<Subject 1>` is the main actor of <Video 1> wearing the outfit of <Picture 1> and holding its pose: the face, hair, skin tone and body come from <Video 1> (describe them briefly as seen in the frames, so they are kept), the OUTFIT and the POSE come from <Picture 1>. List every piece of the outfit: each garment, its color, pattern, fabric and fit, plus shoes, accessories, jewelry, hats, glasses and belts. Then describe the pose of <Picture 1> in plain words: stance, where each arm and hand is and what the fingers do, the head angle, the gaze direction and the attitude.
   - `<Subject 2>` is everything else of <Video 1> that stays: the place, the objects, the props held and the other people.
   - `<Audio 1>` is the synchronized audio track of <Video 1>, reused as it is.
4. `retention_analysis`: <Subject 1> is `attribute_transfer` (the outfit and the pose of <Picture 1> transferred onto the main actor); <Subject 2> is `fully_preserved`; <Video 1> (timing, shots, camera work and pacing) is `fully_preserved`; <Audio 1> is `fully_copy`.
5. `detailed_description`: follow <Video 1> shot by shot with the same shot count, cut times, camera movement, framing and timing. In every shot describe <Subject 1> in the outfit of <Picture 1>, in the pose of <Picture 1>: the body keeps that pose for the whole shot, with only small natural movement on top (breathing, blinking, the head and the mouth following the speech, the cloth moving). The original movements and gestures of the actor in <Video 1> are replaced by the pose of <Picture 1>. Be as explicit as the guide asks.
6. Do NOT describe the original clothing or the original pose of the main actor anywhere, and do not add events, characters, objects or camera moves that are not in <Video 1>.
7. Audio: the original audio of <Video 1> is kept (<Audio 1>, the synchronized audio track of <Video 1>, `fully_copy`). Do not invent words: write a line inside `<d>` only when the request of the user quotes it, with the speaker tag of the person who says it; otherwise say that the people keep speaking, the mouth opening and closing exactly as in <Video 1>, while the body holds the pose. In `overall_soundscape` and `non_diegetic_music` describe the sound of <Audio 1> as it is, even when the new look would sound different.
8. Timing (mandatory): the request lists the exact time of every frame of <Video 1>. Never compute times yourself, copy them from that list. Describe the video as a TIMELINE: after the first sentence of each shot, give a time-stamped action for every visible change, written `At MM:SS.mmm, ...` (small movement, head turn, expression, mouth opening or closing, cloth movement, camera move), using the listed frame times, so that the whole length of <Video 1> is covered from the first to the last frame. A cut is a new `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ...` at the first frame of the new shot.
9. If the request of the user adds a detail, apply it on top of this preset. If it contradicts the main change of this preset, the preset wins.
