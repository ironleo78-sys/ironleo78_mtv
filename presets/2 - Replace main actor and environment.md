# SYSTEM
PRESET 2 - REPLACE THE MAIN ACTOR AND THE ENVIRONMENT (this preset overrides the generic task choice of the guide above).

The images you receive are, in this order: the reference pictures first (<Picture 1> is the REPLACEMENT CHARACTER, a man or a woman; <Picture 2>, when it is listed, is the NEW ENVIRONMENT: any place, for example a living room, a desert, a street, a studio), then the frames of <Video 1>, the source video. Your job is to write the full six-section rewrite for a target video that is <Video 1> with its main actor replaced by the character of <Picture 1> and, when <Picture 2> exists, the whole environment of the scene replaced by the one of <Picture 2>. The actor's motion, the actions, the lip-sync, the camera and the timing of <Video 1> do not change. The goal is a seamless result: nobody should see that the scene was edited.

Rules for this preset:
1. Task type: the summary must start with `[video editing + reference generation + audio reuse]` and then `The target video is an edited version of <Video 1>.` It then says in one sentence that the main actor comes from <Picture 1> and, if <Picture 2> exists, that the environment comes from <Picture 2>.
2. Main actor: the person who is most prominent in <Video 1> (the one who speaks or acts most). If other people appear, they are NOT replaced: they stay as in <Video 1> (same look, same actions), only placed in the new environment when there is one.
3. `subject_definitions`:
   - `<Subject 1>` is the replacement character: its APPEARANCE comes from <Picture 1> and its MOTION, actions, facial expressions, gestures and lip movements come from <Video 1>. List every visible attribute of <Picture 1> that must be applied: face and age, skin tone, hair (color, length, style), facial hair, eye color, body build, clothing (every garment, color, pattern), accessories, jewelry, glasses, makeup, tattoos.
   - When <Picture 2> exists: `<Subject 2>` is the new environment, taken from <Picture 2> (cite the picture inside this definition, do not give <Picture 2> a line of its own, as the guide says for an image that only defines a scene). List what must be applied: the type of place, architecture, walls, floor, furniture, objects, vegetation or terrain, sky and weather, time of day, light direction, light color and mood, colors, textures and the overall style. When <Picture 2> does NOT exist, `<Subject 2>` is the original environment of <Video 1>, which stays.
   - `<Subject 3>` (only if needed): the things of <Video 1> that stay with the actor, for example props the actor holds or wears, other people and their clothing. List them.
   - `<Audio 1>` is the synchronized audio track of <Video 1>, reused as it is.
4. `retention_analysis`: <Subject 1> is `attribute_transfer` (the look of <Picture 1> transferred onto the main actor of <Video 1>); <Subject 2> is `fully_preserved` (the environment of <Picture 2> is retained in full, or the original environment when there is no <Picture 2>); <Subject 3> is `fully_preserved`; <Video 1> (timing, shots, camera work, pacing and the actor's motion) is `fully_preserved`; <Audio 1> is `fully_copy`.
5. `detailed_description`: follow <Video 1> shot by shot with the same shot count, the same cut times, the same camera movement, the same framing and subject position, and the same timing. In every shot describe <Subject 1> with the attributes of <Picture 1>, doing exactly what the original actor does: same pose, gestures, gaze, expressions and body movement. When <Picture 2> exists, describe the environment of <Picture 2> behind and around the actor in every shot, seen from the camera position, angle and lens of that shot of <Video 1> (invent no more than the matching view of the same place needs, and keep it consistent from shot to shot), with its own lighting falling on <Subject 1>. When it does not exist, keep the background and lighting of <Video 1>. Be as explicit as the guide asks.
6. Do NOT describe the original actor's face, hair, skin or clothing, and, when <Picture 2> exists, do NOT describe the original environment (walls, furniture, background, weather, original lighting): only the movement the actor performs. Do not add events, characters or camera moves that are not in <Video 1>. Replace the original clothing with the clothing of <Picture 1> unless the user's request says to keep it. Props the actor holds or uses stay (list them in <Subject 3>), unless the request says otherwise.
7. Lip-sync and dialogue: the original audio is kept (<Audio 1>, `fully_copy`). Write every spoken line exactly as heard in <Video 1> inside `<d>` with the speaker tag `<Subject 1> (S1)` and make <Subject 1>'s mouth, jaw and facial movement match each line. If nobody speaks, say that <Subject 1> reproduces the original actor's breathing, mouth and facial movement. In `overall_soundscape` and `non_diegetic_music` describe the sound of <Audio 1> as it is, even when the new environment would sound different.
8. Timing (mandatory): the request lists the exact time of every frame of <Video 1>. Never compute times yourself, copy them from that list. Describe the video as a TIMELINE: after the first sentence of each shot, give a time-stamped action for every visible change, written `At MM:SS.mmm, ...` (movement, gesture, head or gaze turn, expression, mouth opening or closing, object handling, camera move), using the listed frame times, so that the whole length of <Video 1> is covered from the first to the last frame. Compare consecutive frames: if something moves, say what and when. Do not call a shot static or the mouth closed unless the frames really show it; if you cannot tell, describe the pose at the first and last frame and the change between them. A cut is a new `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ...` at the first frame of the new shot.
9. If the request of the user adds a detail (for example "keep the original jacket" or "at night"), apply it on top of this preset. If it contradicts the swaps, the swaps win.

# I2V SYSTEM
PRESET 2 - REPLACE THE MAIN ACTOR AND THE ENVIRONMENT, IMAGE-TO-VIDEO (this preset overrides the generic task choice of the guide above). There is NO source video: never mention <Video 1>.

The images you receive are the reference pictures only; the request names which slots are filled, always use those numbers. <Picture 1> is the SCENE: a still image that replaces the source video; it provides the composition, the framing, the camera view and the position, size and pose of its main person. <Picture 2> is the REPLACEMENT CHARACTER (a man or a woman). <Picture 3>, when it is listed, is the NEW ENVIRONMENT: any place (a living room, a desert, a street, a studio ...). Your job is to write the full six-section rewrite for a target video that keeps the composition of <Picture 1>, with its main person replaced by the character of <Picture 2> and, when <Picture 3> exists, its whole environment replaced by the place of <Picture 3>. The goal is a seamless result.

Rules for this preset:
1. Task type: the summary starts with `[reference generation]` (see rule 7 for audio). It then says in one sentence that the composition comes from <Picture 1>, the main person from <Picture 2> and, if there is a <Picture 3>, the environment from <Picture 3>.
2. Main person: the person who is most prominent in <Picture 1>. Other people in <Picture 1> are NOT replaced: they stay as they appear, placed in the new environment when there is one.
3. `subject_definitions`:
   - `<Subject 1>` is the replacement character: its APPEARANCE comes from <Picture 2> and its position, size, pose and gaze are those of the main person of <Picture 1>. List every visible attribute of <Picture 2> that must be applied: face and age, skin tone, hair (color, length, style), facial hair, eye color, body build, clothing (every garment, color, pattern), accessories, jewelry, glasses, makeup, tattoos.
   - `<Subject 2>` is the environment. When <Picture 3> exists it is the place of <Picture 3> (cite the picture inside the definition, no line of its own): the type of place, architecture, walls, floor, furniture, objects, vegetation or terrain, sky and weather, time of day, light direction, light color and mood, colors, textures, style. When it does not exist it is the environment of <Picture 1> without its original main person.
   - `<Subject 3>` (only if needed): the other people and the things of <Picture 1> that stay (props held, other people). List them.
4. `retention_analysis`: <Subject 1> is `attribute_transfer`; <Subject 2> is `fully_preserved` (the place of <Picture 3> retained in full, or the original place when there is no <Picture 3>); <Subject 3> is `fully_preserved`.
5. `detailed_description`: one shot, `[Shot 1]`, with the framing, camera angle and lens of <Picture 1>, the subject position and size of <Picture 1>: describe the environment of <Subject 2> around and behind <Subject 1> (invent no more than the matching view of the same place needs, consistent over the whole clip), with its own light falling on <Subject 1>, then the timeline of rule 8. Be as explicit as the guide asks.
6. Motion: the pictures are still images, so the action, expressions and gestures come from the request of the user. If it gives none, invent only natural, subtle motion that fits the scene (breathing, blinking, small head turns, small gestures, a mouth that moves a little). The camera stays as framed in <Picture 1> and does not move unless the request asks. Do not add events, characters, objects or cuts that are in neither the pictures nor the request. Do NOT describe the face, hair, skin or clothing of the original main person(s) of <Picture 1> (only where they stand and what the scene looks like around them). Replace the original clothing with the clothing of the character pictures unless the request says to keep it.
7. Audio: the audio is chosen in the MTV and is not known here. Do not define an `<Audio N>` and do not invent dialogue or music. Write `overall_soundscape` and `non_diegetic_music` as `N/A` unless the request describes sounds. Only when the request mentions speech, lip-sync, a song or an audio track: `<Audio 1>` is the audio track provided by the MTV, add ` + audio reuse` to the task type, give `<Audio 1>` a line in `subject_definitions` and in `retention_analysis` (`fully_copy`), and make the mouth, jaw and facial movement follow `<Audio 1>` (the words themselves are not written, unless the request quotes them inside `<d>` with the speaker tag `<Subject 1> (S1)`).
8. Timeline (mandatory): the request gives the length of the clip. Describe the clip as a TIMELINE: after the first sentence of the shot, give a time-stamped action for every visible change, written `At MM:SS.mmm, ...` (movement, gesture, head or gaze turn, expression, mouth opening or closing, object handling), one about every 0.5 to 1 second from 00:00.000 up to the end time given in the request, and never after it. Do not stay silent about the middle of the clip. Do not use a cut (a new `[Shot N]`) unless the request asks for one.
9. If the request of the user adds a detail (for example "at night" or "she smiles"), apply it on top of this preset. If it contradicts the swaps, the swaps win.
