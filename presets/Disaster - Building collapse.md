The change of this preset happens to the world, the image, the camera or the action around them, never to who they are. Their performance also stays exactly as in <Video 1>: the same acting, facial expressions, gestures, body movement, positions and mouth movement (lip-sync), frame for frame. Only what is not the characters changes.

CAMERA (mandatory): the camera is FIXED to the camera of <Video 1>: the same position, framing, lens, cut times and only the camera movement that <Video 1> itself has. Do not add any pan, tilt, zoom, push-in, pull-back, orbit, crane, shake or cut to show the change of this preset; the change appears inside the existing frames (in the background, the sky, the light, around the people). Only a camera move named in the request of the user may be added.

2. `subject_definitions`:
   - `<Subject 1>` is the collapse: a distant tower crumbling, a huge cloud of dust rolling outward far away, debris falling in the distance.
   - `<Subject 2>` is the content of <Video 1> that stays: the people, the place, the objects and their motion (the people in full detail, see ACTORS).
   - `<Audio 1>` is the synchronized audio track of <Video 1>, reused as it is.
3. `retention_analysis`: <Subject 1> is `fully_preserved`; <Subject 2> is `partially_preserved` (the same content in the new scene); <Video 1> (timing, shots, camera work, pacing and motion) is `fully_preserved`; <Audio 1> is `fully_copy`.
4. `detailed_description`: begin with one or two sentences that set the disaster, then follow <Video 1> shot by shot with the same shot count, cut times, camera movement, framing, positions and timing, describing it in every shot, kept consistent from shot to shot. It starts far away and grows; its peak comes in the second half of the clip, always behind or around the main characters. Be as explicit as the guide asks.
5. Do not change anyone's identity, clothing or position, and do not add characters or camera moves that are not in <Video 1>: only the world around the people changes (wet hair and clothes in rain, snow on shoulders, wind in the hair are fine).
6. SAFETY OF THE MAIN CHARACTERS (mandatory): the event is dramatic and spectacular, but it happens AROUND them, mostly in the background and at a distance. It never touches, hits, burns, buries, injures or endangers the main characters: they stay unharmed, in the same clothing, keep their performance, positions and lip-sync, and at most glance at it, squint, or lean into the wind for a moment. No victims, no injuries, no blood, no one in danger on screen.
7. Audio: the original audio of <Video 1> is kept (<Audio 1>, the synchronized audio track of <Video 1>, `fully_copy`). Do not invent words: write a line inside `<d>` only when the request of the user quotes it, with the speaker tag of the person who says it; otherwise say that the people keep speaking, singing and moving their mouths exactly as in <Video 1>. In `overall_soundscape` and `non_diegetic_music` describe the sound of <Audio 1> as it is, even when the new scene would sound different (no added thunder, wind, crowd or explosion sounds).
8. Timing (mandatory): the request lists the exact time of every frame of <Video 1>. Never compute times yourself, copy them from that list. Describe the video as a TIMELINE: after the first sentence of each shot, give a time-stamped action for every visible change, written `At MM:SS.mmm, ...` (movement, gesture, head or gaze turn, expression, mouth opening or closing, object handling, camera move), using the listed frame times, so that the whole length of <Video 1> is covered from the first to the last frame. Compare consecutive frames: if something moves, say what and when. Do not call a shot static or the mouth closed unless the frames really show it; if you cannot tell, describe the pose at the first and last frame and the change between them. A cut is a new `[Shot N] At MM:SS.mmm, ...` at the first frame of the new shot.
9. If the request of the user adds a detail, apply it on top of this preset. If it contradicts the main change of this preset, the preset wins.
